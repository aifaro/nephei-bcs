    The drill sergeant's tips

    What nobody tells you on day one

    Classic recruit mistakes. Learn them before your first game and you will save more than one battalion.

    01

    Look after your MSR! Combat Trains off the map are -3 to SNAFU, and on a Fail the formation does not activate. Lesson 3

    02

    No OBJ, no attack. Regular and Shock Attacks, Attacks by Fire and Barrages all need an OBJ Zone; only Engagements are fought without one. Lesson 3

    03

    Tanks do not shoot their way into villages. An Engagement takes no ground: to keep the hex you need a Regular or Shock Attack. Lesson 5

    04

    Three is a crowd. Stacking more than 2 units in one hex is legal, but it gives adverse modifiers and extra rolls to the enemy artillery. Lesson 4

    05

    Count your Fire Events. Each unit has two per activation, and spending the last one STOPs it, even with MA left. Lesson 1

    06

    Not every ZOC stops everyone. Tanks ignore normal ZOCs; trucks stop even in an Engagement Zone. Lesson 4

    Briefing · What the BCS is

    Mechanized war at battalion scale

    Designed by Dean Essig for The Gamers / MMP, the BCS covers the late First World War to the present day. Each counter is usually a battalion, each hex is 1 to 1.6 km across and each turn is a day.

    01 · FORMATIONS

    You play by command

    Nothing moves on its own: every unit belongs to a formation with its HQ, trains and artillery. They share SNAFU, fatigue, Support and Prepared Defense.

    02 · ACTIVATIONS

    Alternating turns

    The players take turns activating one formation at a time. With luck, the formation goes again straight away (Second Activation) before the enemy can react.

    03 · SNAFU

    Logistics rule

    Every activation starts with 2d6 on the SNAFU Table. A cut supply route or badly placed trains sink the roll, and on a Fail the formation does not activate.

    04 · COMBINED ARMS

    The right tool for the job

    Infantry takes ground, armor fights armor, Support is spread among the battalions and artillery suppresses. Choosing well is the key.
