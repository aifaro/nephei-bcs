2.5k–2.5n · Formation Activation markers

Activation chits

Instead of alternating by choice, formations come out of a cup. Each formation has its chit; when it is Done, its chit is set aside until next turn. 2.5k

Random 2.5l

All available chits go in the cup; draw one and that formation activates. Pure chance: the same side can come up several times in a row.

Modified, three at a time 2.5m

Both sides' chits in one cup. Draw three: the side owning the majority picks which of them activates, and the other two go back. With only one or two chits left, draw one and that one activates.

Smoothing 2.5n

No shared cup. The side with more formations ("Large") draws a numbered chit each time it is its turn: on a 1 it activates one formation; on a 2, two in a row. There are as many chits as the other side has formations, with as many "2"s as the difference between the two.

    2.4 · Orders

    Orders

    In the Orders Phase each player secretly writes one order per formation. Orders control the HQ, not the units: they operate normally inside Command Radius. 2.4

    HQ Move

    HQ Move

    The route the HQ will follow: waypoints and end hex. When the formation activates, a March OBJ goes on the end hex and the HQ heads for it along that route without straying. It can stop short, but it cannot change direction. 2.4b

    No Orders

    No Orders

    The HQ does not move, except for a Voluntary Retreat. The units, though, do as they please. It is the default order. 2.4c

    Prepared Defense

    Prepared Defense

    When it activates, the formation must create or keep its PD, and cannot back out. The HQ cannot make a Voluntary Retreat. If it loses the PD, it defaults to No Orders. 2.4d

    Fatigue Recovery

    Fatigue Recovery

    The formation commits to a Recovery Activation. With Orders there is no Failure Flip. It is the only order that can be combined: PD + Fatigue Recovery. 2.4e

    Changing orders

    Only in the Orders Phase, and free: cross it out and write the new one. Orders stay in force for as many turns as you like. Exception: if the MSR is blocked this turn, you can give the HQ a new HQ Move order at that moment. 2.4f

    March OBJ

    It goes in any road hex, even with enemy units in it. It creates no OBJ Zone and triggers no fatigue roll, and Recon units cannot create one. 2.4g

    March Routing optional

    Extra realism within the Orders rules: the designer himself asks you not to use it if you would play the route to "pick up" units along the way. If the March OBJ lies beyond Command Radius, units can only enter hexes within 2 of the route, unless they are already inside the radius, follow the route to its end, retreat, advance after combat or are reinforcements. 2.4h

    4.7i · Soft Jumps

    Soft Jumps

    Without this rule, every Jump on an HQ is a sure disaster. With it, sometimes the enemy reaches where it thought the HQ was… and finds nobody: its situation map was wrong. Roll one die per Jumped hex, before resolving any retreats. 4.7i

    1-4 · Soft Jump

    • The owner moves the HQ and its trains (whichever was Jumped) wherever desired, with no path or minimum distance.
    • The hexes must have suitable terrain, no enemy units, EZOCs or Engagement Zones, and must not block an enemy MSR. They need not be Legal at that moment.
    • It keeps its markers (Prepared Defense, Coordination) without adding any, and the trains stay Ghost or not, as they were.

    5-6 · Hard Jump

    • The HQ goes 3 or more hexes away, to a Legal hex outside any Engagement Zone.
    • Its trains flip to Ghost; it loses its Prepared Defense and gets Coordination.
    • Jumped trains go back 10 hexes along their MSR. See lesson 3.
    Utilidad

    Soft Jump or Hard Jump?

    When the enemy enters the hex of an HQ or trains, roll here before resolving retreats.

    [Optional]

    Other optional rules

    The rest of the series' optional rules, for when you have mastered the basics. Some games make one of them mandatory: always check their own rules.

    Planned Fatigue 6.4

    A complete package, devised by Jim Stravers: use all of it or none of it. Fatigue is no longer rolled; it becomes a currency you spend to buy things.

    FatigueBuys
    1An extra OBJ (not Recon)
    1An OBJ more than 15 hexes from the HQ (1 in total if it is also an extra one)
    1Removing a Traffic marker (free at the end of the Activities Phase)
    2Forced March: MA ×3, with no combat, OBJs or Second Activation
    1+2 to the Second Activation roll

    SNAFU only counts Fresh (+1) and Fat-4 (-1). A Full result gives a single free OBJ; Partial and Fail give none. At Fat-4 nothing can be bought and the formation must recover.

    Unit Traffic 4.0e

    Any hex with units that are not deployed and active (and always one with an HQ) negates roads for whoever enters: they pay the normal terrain cost. Very realistic and very slow; the designer suggests adding it later.

    Realistic Movement 4.9

    No moving, undoing and trying again: if a counter enters a hex, it really did. Honest mistakes are put right by talking it through with your opponent.

    Long-range Engagements 5.2f

    Beyond one hex, a "Both Loss" result becomes "No Effect".

    Stand Off AV vs. Red Support 6.5

    When a Stand Off AV unit (or a host with Stand Off Support) is attacked by a unit with Red Support, the defender first fires at the attacker's Support, on the "Target is Support" part of the Engagement Table. If it Drops it, the attacker loses its Support DRM for that attack.
