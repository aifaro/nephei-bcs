Chapter 3 · Command and supply

Command, supply and objectives

In lesson 2 you saw that everything happens formation by formation and that SNAFU decides how well each one works. Here you will see what it depends on. In the BCS nothing moves on its own: every unit belongs to a formation, depends on its HQ and lives off the route that links it to the trains and the rear. Cutting those threads does more damage than many attacks.

Formations

The piece that activates

A formation is a command with its HQ, its Combat Trains and its units: a division in one game, a brigade or a Kampfgruppe in another. The turn revolves around them, one at a time, alternating sides. 1.3

What they share

Intrinsic and independent

Intrinsic units have their formation printed to the right of the symbol and never change command. Independent units and Arty Points are assigned in the Assignment Phase and, while assigned, are just another part of their formation. 1.3b · 2.3

The Blob

The formation's area: the smooth shape drawn by its outermost units (ignoring trains, Out of Command units and reinforcements). If an active unit of another friendly formation moves inside, both become Mixed: Coordination and -1 to SNAFU. 1.3c · 3.2d

Command Radius

How far the HQ reaches

The Command Radius is printed on the HQ. Count hexes from the HQ to the unit, ignoring terrain, ZOCs and enemy units. Beyond it, the unit is Out of Command. 1.4

Out of Command

  • It suffers isolation each time its formation activates.
  • It can still make Regular and Shock Attacks, and artillery can fire with the proper OBJ Zones and spotters.
  • If it begins the phase Out of Command, it cannot spot for barrages, Jump HQs or trains, or move to cut an enemy MSR that has no alternative.

Back to the fold

Units (except trains) must end each activation inside the radius. If they cannot, they must move or attack in a way that brings them closer, or stay put. 1.4e

The radius does not limit OBJ placement, Advance after Combat or barrages. Unassigned independents have no radius.

Prepared Defense

The formation's posture

Prepared Defense is not just digging holes: it is planned protective fires and firing positions. It is a status of the whole formation, trading freedom of action for a better but positional defense. 1.7

To enter it 1.7a

  • At least one Arty Point (before SNAFU is applied).
  • No MSR Blocked marker.
  • In the Initial Activation, not the Second.

What it gives 1.7c

  • +1 when defending on the Combat Table and the Engagement Table.
  • Only adjacent Firers can engage it.
  • A better row on the Barrage Table.
  • Against a Situational Retreat it holds the hex (each unit loses a step).

What it costs

  • SNAFU is never better than Partial, whatever you roll.
  • Otherwise the formation moves, fights, recovers and goes for a Second Activation normally.

Who uses it and when it is lost 1.7b · 1.7d

Units on their Deployed-side and inside Command Radius use it. It is lost if the HQ moves or is Jumped, if an MSR Blocked marker arrives, if the formation runs out of artillery or if the player removes it.

HQs & Combat Trains

HQs and Combat Trains

Neither fights, and neither can be destroyed, attacked or barraged. But if the enemy enters their hex (Jumps them), they retreat in disorder and the formation pays for it in SNAFU. 4.7

Combat Trains

They "move" by being placed straight into a Legal hex that completes the MSR. If they move, or end up less than 5 or more than 15 hexes from the HQ, they flip to their Ghost side (-1 to SNAFU).

They recover if they are in a Legal hex of a Complete MSR, at Optimal Distance and did not move in the previous Activities Phase. 4.7b · 4.7c

Main Supply Route

The Complete MSR

A chain of Legal hexes that runs, in this order, from the HQ to its own trains and from the trains to a supply source. It is checked in every Preparation Phase. 3.1

Legal hex 3.1a

  • It has a Primary or Secondary Road. A Track only counts between the HQ and the trains (and brings the Track DRM on the SNAFU roll).
  • It is connected to a supply source by Legal hexes, or is one.
  • It does not block an enemy formation's only MSR.
  • It cannot hold enemy units or EZOCs. A friendly unit negates the EZOC, but only so the HQ can be there.

If it is cut

With no Complete MSR in the Preparation Phase, the trains go off-map: -3 to SNAFU. If they were already off-map, the formation gets an MSR Blocked marker, which can worsen to level 2.

The trains come back to any Legal hex that completes the MSR during the Activities Phase, as Ghost.

What does not affect it

Engagement Zones and Screens do not cut the MSR. What cuts it is enemy units and their ZOCs on the road. Once traced, the HQ–trains leg does not change until one of them moves, the enemy cuts it or they are Jumped.

Objectives

OBJs: where you can attack

OBJs mark where the formation will operate this activation. SNAFU says how many: 2 on Full, 1 on Partial and none on Fail. They go in a hex with an enemy unit (not Support) or on a Victory hex, at any distance from the HQ. 3.3

The OBJ Zone 3.3a

Each OBJ creates a zone with a 2-hex radius. Inside it you can make:

✓Regular Attacks
✓Shock Attacks
✓Attacks by Fire
✓Barrages
✓Moves into a Victory hex*
—Engagements: not needed

* To take a Victory hex, the OBJ must be in that very hex.

  • Double OBJ. Two SNAFU OBJs in the same hex: +1 on the Combat Table and the Engagement Table against whatever is inside.
  • Recon. A reconnaissance unit can create an extra OBJ by spending a Fire Event and rolling 1d6 ≤ its AR. It cannot be STOPPED or create a Double OBJ. 3.3f
  • Once placed they do not move, and they are all removed in the Clean Up Phase. They are not saved for another activation.
Safe Paths · Isolation

The thread to the HQ

A Safe Path is a route of contiguous hexes from each unit to its HQ, free of enemy units and of EZOCs that affect its Movement Class, and no longer than Command Radius + 5. In or next to the HQ there always is one. At this scale, losing it is worse than losing the MSR. 1.9

What it is needed for

  • Flipping from the Leg side to the Truck or Tac side.
  • Removing a Dropped Support marker.
  • Taking replacements.
  • Avoiding isolation.
  • Retreating successfully (Leg or Truck).

What cuts it

  • Enemy units.
  • For Leg and Truck, any EZOC; for Tac, only Real AV ZOCs.
  • Friendly units negate EZOCs.

STOP Terrain, Support, Engagement Zones and Screens do not cut it.

Which class is needed

The unit's own, except to flip from Leg to Truck or Tac (the new side's class), to establish Support or take AV replacements (Tac) and non-AV replacements (Leg). Unassigned independents and units on the map edge always have one.

Jumping HQs & Combat Trains

Jumping an HQ

Table results do not affect HQs or trains, but if an enemy unit enters their hex (moving or advancing after combat), it Jumps them and they must retreat. 4.7d

The Jumped HQ 4.7e

  • It is placed 3 or more hexes away, in a Legal hex outside any Engagement Zone.
  • Its trains flip to Ghost.
  • It loses its Prepared Defense.
  • It gets a Coordination marker.

It can escape into a pocket: no Complete MSR or minimum distance needed, but it must end in a hex with one of its own units. 4.7f

Jumped trains 4.7h

They are placed in a Legal hex that completes the MSR, at least 10 hexes back along their MSR's road, and flip to Ghost. If there is no such hex, they go off-map.

Optional rule: on a 1-4 the Jump is soft and the owner relocates them as desired. 4.7i

Who cannot Jump

  • Unprepared units.
  • Retreating units.
  • Screening units.
  • Units that began the phase Out of Command.
  • Other HQs and trains.
