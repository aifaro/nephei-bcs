What the BCS is

Combined arms at battalion scale

The Battalion Combat Series is a series by The Gamers, designed by Dean Essig and published by Multi-Man Publishing (MMP). It covers mechanized war from the Second World War to the present day: Normandy, Lorraine, Sicily, the Golan Heights… Each game in the series is a different battle with the same core rules. 1.0

1 counteris usually a battalion (sometimes a company)
1 hexis 1 to 1.6 km, depending on the game
1 turnis a day of operations
2 sidestaking turns formation by formation

The Series Rules are common to every game. They are what this academy teaches: units, sequence, command, movement and combat. The full rulebook is free: BCS v2.0 Series Rules (PDF).

The four ideas

What makes the BCS different

Keep these four ideas in mind and every rule you read afterwards will make sense.

01 · FORMATIONS

You play by command

Nothing moves on its own: every unit belongs to a formation with its HQ, trains and artillery. They share SNAFU, fatigue, Support and Prepared Defense.

02 · ACTIVATIONS

Alternating turns

The players take turns activating one formation at a time. If it chooses, the formation can try to go again straight away (Second Activation) before the enemy reacts, but every extra effort tires it.

03 · SNAFU

Logistics rule

Every activation starts with 2d6 on the SNAFU Table. A cut supply route or badly placed trains sink the roll, and on a Fail the formation does not activate.

04 · COMBINED ARMS

The right tool for the job

Infantry takes ground, armor fights armor, Support is spread among the battalions and artillery suppresses. Choosing well is the key.

Orders

Optional: each formation's orders are written down.

First player

2d6 per side: the higher roll activates first.

Activations

Formations alternate until all are Done. Almost everything happens here.

Turn end

HQs return to their unused side and the turn marker advances.

An activation, in one line

When it is your turn, you pick one of your formations and do this, always in this order:

1 SNAFU2 Place OBJs3 Move and fight4 Does fatigue rise?5 Second Activation? optional

SNAFU says how well the formation works today; the OBJs, where it can attack; and the Second Activation, whether it goes again before the enemy can react. Going again is a choice, not a duty: every extra activation risks fatigue, and a tired formation performs worse and worse. Then it is your opponent's turn.
