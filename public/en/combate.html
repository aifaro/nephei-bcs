Chapter 5 · Combat

Five ways to fight

You know how to move and what stops you (lesson 4), and that attacking needs an OBJ Zone (lesson 3) and often a Fire Event (lesson 2). Now, to combat. The BCS has no single combat table. Each tool has its own table, requirements and cost, and no unit can use them all. Learning to combine them is learning to play.

01 · REGULAR ATTACK

Regular Attack

Who
Attack-Capable units (infantry and Dual)
Against
Any adjacent enemy hex
Table
Combat Table · 2d6 + DRM
Cost
No Fire Events, but the Attack and Assist units become FINISHED
Artillery
Up to 3 points (destruction) or 1 for suppression (+2)
Rule
5.1
Try it in the simulator

The classic assault: a battalion, with its artillery and an Assist unit, tries to throw the enemy out of a hex. It is the only way for foot infantry to do damage on its own, and the best way to take ground.

Attack sequence 5.1b

  1. The attacker declares the attack and the defending hex.
  2. The defender announces whether it can use Prepared Defense.
  3. The defender picks which unit gives its Action Rating.
  4. The attacker picks the Attack unit and, if desired, an Assist.
  5. The attacker carries out any Barrage.
  6. Work out the net DRM: the attacker's AR and modifiers, minus the defender's.
  7. Roll 2d6 on the Combat Table.
  8. Apply losses, retreats and the advance.

Requirements

  • The Attack unit is Attack-Capable, adjacent, could enter the hex in normal movement, has an MA above 0 and is neither FINISHED nor over-stacked. It may be STOPPED.
  • The defending hex is in an OBJ Zone. Command Radius does not limit attacks.
  • A hex can only take one Regular Attack per phase (but if its units retreat, they can be attacked again by other units).
  • The Assist (one at most) is Attack-Capable or shows an AV, is adjacent to the defender and to the Attack unit (or stacked with it), and gives +1.

Worked example

Attacking infantry, AR3 Red AV Support from its formation+1 Suppression Barrage+2 Assist by a tank battalion+1 Attacker total7 Defending infantry, AR3 In Prepared Defense, in woods (Terrain)+1 +1 Defender total5 Net DRM: 7 − 5+2

What can happen

  • A1, A2: the attacker loses steps, always from the Attack unit (never from Support or the Assist).
  • [A1]: it loses a step only if the defender is in Prepared Defense.
  • D1, D2: the defender loses steps, the first from the unit that gave the AR.
  • Situational: a 3-hex retreat or, if the defender is in Prepared Defense or on Key Terrain, it stays but each unit loses a step.
  • Retreat: a full retreat; Leg infantry goes back to its HQ.
  • If the hex is emptied, the Attack unit must advance. The Assist can only do so if it was stacked with it.
02 · SHOCK ATTACK

Shock Attack

Who
A single Tac MA unit: Attack-Capable, Red AV, Light AV or Breakthrough armor
Against
A hex with no AV at all, Real or Support, costing 3 MPs or less
Table
Combat Table · 2d6 + DRM
Cost
1 Fire Event. Not FINISHED afterwards
Artillery
1 point at most: suppression +1 or destruction
Rule
5.1k · 5.1m
Try it in the simulator

Blitzkrieg on a counter: a mechanized unit uses its armor and speed to overrun a position with no anti-tank defense and keeps moving afterwards. If it wins, it advances into the hex for free; if it fails and still has MPs and a Fire Event, it can try again against the same hex.

Differences from the Regular Attack

  • The same hex can be Shock Attacked as many times as you can manage, even by the same unit.
  • No Assist is allowed. The unit cannot be STOPPED and needs at least 1 MP and 1 Fire Event.
  • Suppression gives +1 (not +2). If the same unit first made an Attack by Fire against that hex and still has a Fire Event, add another +1.
  • Breakthrough armor adds +2; a Hard Red AV, +1 (only one of the two).
  • Each new Shock Attack starts with its modifiers at zero: you have to earn them again.
  • Support protects: a hex with any AV, even Support, cannot be Shock Attacked.

Shock Attacks exploit gaps; they do not break fronts. Against a hex with an anti-tank gun, even in Support, first strip the Support with an Engagement (a Drop result) or make a Regular Attack.

03 · ENGAGEMENT

Engagement

Who
Real AV units, except Light AV and Screening units
Against
An AV unit or a host's Support, within range
Table
Engagement Table · 2d6 + DRM
Cost
1 Fire Event. No MP cost
OBJ Zone
Not needed
Rule
5.2
Try it in the simulator

Armor against armor. It takes no ground: it destroys enemy AV units, strips a hex of its Support (opening it to your Shock Attacks) and frees you from an AV ZOC that has stopped you. It can be done at any point in a unit's move, and the unit can keep moving afterwards if it has Fire Events left.

How it is worked out

Each side adds its AV + Action Rating. If the target is Support, use the Support's AV and the host's AR. The net DRM is Firer minus target.

ModifierDRMApplies to
Target in a Double OBJ Zone+1Firer
The Firer is Limited AV−1Firer
Prepared Defense and/or several Supports in range+1Target
Stand Off target not in Support+1Target

Range

  • The target must be within the Firer's range; otherwise there is no shot.
  • If the Firer is beyond the target's range, results against the Firer are ignored. That is why long guns are so valuable.
  • If the target is in Terrain or Prepared Defense, the maximum range is 1 hex.
  • Beyond 1 hex you need a line of sight with no Blocking Terrain between the two hexes.

Who is the target

The defender chooses, among the hex's units with the highest AV or range, in this order: first Real AV units; then the Support of a formation with several Supports; last, the Support of a formation with only one. A Drop result strips every unit in the hex of its Support.

04 · ATTACK BY FIRE

Attack by Fire

Who
Real AV units (Red, Limited, Stand Off, Light and Dual)
Against
An adjacent hex with no AV, Real or Support, in an OBJ Zone
Table
Barrage Table, Attack by Fire column · 1d6 per unit
Cost
1 Fire Event
Rule
5.3
Try it in the simulator

The tank fires high explosive at the infantry. It works like a one-point Barrage, but with its own, less lethal column on the table. Unlike a Barrage there is no limit: you can make as many as you like against the same hex, in any order with your attacks.

  • Each Attack by Fire is made by a single unit and resolved separately; it is never added to Arty Points.
  • The Firer may be STOPPED but not FINISHED. If it entered a ZOC requiring a Stopping Engagement, it must win that first.
  • It does not count as suppression for a Regular Attack, but it does give +1 to an immediate Shock Attack by the same unit.
05 · BARRAGE

Barrage

Who
The formation's Arty Points and the side's Air Points
Against
An enemy hex in an OBJ Zone, with a spotter
Table
Barrage Table, Arty column · 1d6 per point and per unit
Cost
The points used (artillery is renewed every activation; air is not)
Rule
5.4
Try it in the simulator

Two very different uses. Suppression spends a point within an attack and gives +2 (or +1 in a Shock Attack) without causing losses: the enemy keeps its head down. Destruction rolls on the Barrage Table to cause losses, but gives no attack DRM.

DestructionMaximum points
Within a Regular Attack3
Within a Shock Attack1
Outside any attack (the spotter is STOPPED)1
  • Only one Barrage per hex per activation, destruction or suppression (in Urban hexes, one destruction Barrage and as many suppression ones as you like).
  • Points are declared before rolling. Roll one die per point and per unit in the hex: 3 points against 2 units is 6 dice. Support never takes losses.
  • The spotter must belong to the active formation (the HQ or trains will do), have begun the phase inside Command Radius, be within 4 hexes and have a line of sight. It may be STOPPED or FINISHED.
  • If a Destruction Barrage within an attack wipes out the hex, the attacker advances without rolling on the Combat Table.

Barrage Table row: Hard units, deployed Stand Off units and Urban hexes are only hit on a 6; City on 5-6; Terrain or Prepared Defense on 4-6; everything else on 3-6. If several apply, use the one best for the target. See the table.

Stopping Engagements

Shooting your way through armor

A Tac MA unit STOPs on entering an AV ZOC. With a Stopping Engagement it can waive that STOP and keep moving. 4.4

Is it required?

Yes, if the AV ZOC comes from a Real AV unit or a host with Stand Off Support: you must fire at least once, even if you do not want to go on. If it only comes from hosts with other Support, it is optional.

When do I win?

When no AV ZOC is left in your hex: because you destroyed, forced back or stripped of Support (Drop) whoever generated it. Each shot costs a Fire Event and, when you spend the last one, you STOP anyway.

Exceptions

Light AV and Screening units cannot make a required Stopping Engagement: they become FINISHED. The defender chooses the target among the units contributing the ZOC. There are never Stopping Engagements during retreats or Advance after Combat.
