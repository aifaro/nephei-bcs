Chapter 1 · Counters and units

How to read a counter

On a BCS counter every number has its place, and the color and style of each figure matter too. The golden rule: what a unit can do depends on the side it is showing right now.

Anatomy

Touch each number

    Two sides

    Deployed or on the move

    The side with the lower MA is the Deployed-side; the one with the higher MA is the Move-side. Flipping costs no MPs, but it is only done before the unit starts acting, and once per activation. Click a counter to flip it. 1.1c · 4.0b

    Unit types

    Infantry, AV and Dual

    Two features set the type: whether the side shows the assault arrow and whether it has an AV. That tells you which tables it fights on. 1.1e

    Infantry arrow · no AV

    Any unit with no printed AV counts as "infantry" for terrain. With the assault arrow it is Attack-Capable: it takes ground with Regular Attacks, and it is the only kind that can be a host for armored Support.

    If it also has a Tac MA (mechanized), it can make Shock Attacks.

    AV units AV · no arrow

    Tanks, tank destroyers and guns. They cannot be the Attack unit in a Regular Attack, but they can Assist. They fight other AV units on the Engagement Table and shoot at infantry with an Attack by Fire.

    They project an AV ZOC, and an Engagement Zone out to their range.

    Dual arrow + AV

    Armored reconnaissance, panzergrenadiers with organic armor… They have it all: they attack on the Combat Table (with +1 for being Dual), engage and make Attacks by Fire. In exchange they cannot take Support.

    Unprepared no arrow · no AV

    A side with no arrow and no AV: infantry on the march or a limbered gun. It has no ZOC, does not attack or Assist, cannot use Support or Prepared Defense and cannot Jump HQs. It can still spot for artillery. 1.1d

    Armor Value

    The four colors of armor

    The large number at the bottom left is the AV. Its style shows the type, and the type changes what the unit can do. 1.1e

    4

    Red AV red

    The offensive armor: tanks. It makes Shock Attacks, Engagements, Attacks by Fire, and Recon if it is a reconnaissance unit.

    • In Support, it gives +1 to its hosts' attack and defense.
    Sherman (3, range 2) · long-barrel Panzer IV (4, range 2) · Panther (5, range 3) · T-34/85 (4, range 2)
    3

    Limited AV outlined

    More defensive: tank destroyers and assault guns. It fires at -1 as the Firer on the Engagement Table and makes no Shock Attacks (unless it is Breakthrough armor).

    • In Support, it only helps the defense.
    M10 (3, range 2) · M18 Hellcat (3, range 3) · SU-85 (4, range 2, Hard) · M36 Jackson (4, range 3)
    5

    Stand Off AV black

    Long-range guns that are easy to hide, like the 88. +1 when they are the target of an Engagement and, when deployed, only a 6 hurts them on the Barrage Table.

    • In Support, they help the defense and force a Stopping Engagement on anyone entering their host's ZOC.
    88 mm L/56 (4, range 3) · 88 mm L/70 (5, range 3) · Jagdtiger (8, range 3)
    0

    Light AV white

    Vehicle-mounted machine guns: armored cars and tankettes. They cannot start Engagements, only be engaged. They do make Shock Attacks and Attacks by Fire.

    • In Support they work just like Limited AV.
    L3/35 (0) · Sd.Kfz. 221 (0) · Marmon-Herrington (0) · Flammpanzer III (3, Hard)

    Hard yellow symbol

    Well-protected vehicles. On the Barrage Table only a 6 affects them. If they are Red AV, they defend at +2 in open terrain and add +1 when making a Shock Attack. Open-topped armor and Second World War mechanized infantry are not Hard.

    Breakthrough breakthrough

    Very heavy armor for cracking strong defenses: Tiger, King Tiger, IS-2, Brummbär. They can always make Shock Attacks, even if their AV is Limited, and add +2 when they do.

    The Range is the small figure at the bottom, between the AV and the MA. If there is none, the range is 1 hex. Above it sits the Action Rating, from 0 to 5.

    Support

    Support: armor spread out

    Instead of sitting on the map, an AV unit can be split into platoons among its formation's infantry. It takes no hex and does not stack: it is kept by the HQ with its marker. 1.5

    Who uses it

    The hosts: the formation's Attack-Capable infantry (assigned independents included). A single Support serves every host at once, however many there are and however many steps it has left.

    Dual units, Unprepared units, units in terrain prohibited to Tac MA and units with Dropped Support cannot use it.

    What it gives

    • An AV ZOC for the host, which stops enemy tanks.
    • +1 on the Combat Table: when attacking, only with Red AV Support; when defending, with any type.
    • Its own AV to receive Engagements. The Support takes the losses, never the host.
    • Protection: a hex with Support cannot be hit by Shock Attacks or Attacks by Fire.

    Units enter and leave Support only in the Assignment Phase. Units marked "Support" on the counter are always in Support.

    Markers

    The ones you will see most

    Markers are not units: they do not fight, do not stack and nothing affects them.
